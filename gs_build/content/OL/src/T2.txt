Q: Who wrote the *Declaration of the Rights of Woman and of the Female Citizen* (1791)?
A: Mary Wollstonecraft | Olympe de Gouges | Harriet Taylor Mill | Hubertine Auclert
K: B | Olympe de Gouges (1791).

Q: Mary Wollstonecraft's *Vindication* (1792) answered the ideas on girls' education in:
A: Rousseau's *Émile* | Locke's *Two Treatises* | Mill's *Subjection* | None of these
K: A | Rousseau's *Émile* (1762).

Q: Who first called herself a "feminist", in 1882?
A: Olympe de Gouges | Hubertine Auclert | Simone de Beauvoir | Clara Zetkin
K: B | Hubertine Auclert (1882).

Q: The "wave" metaphor for feminism was popularised in 1968 by:
A: Rebecca Walker | Martha Weinman Lear | Betty Friedan | Gloria Steinem
K: B | Martha Weinman Lear, "The Second Feminist Wave" (1968).

Q: "The personal is political" is the title of an essay by:
A: Kate Millett | Carol Hanisch | Shulamith Firestone | Adrienne Rich
K: B | Carol Hanisch (written 1969, published 1970).

Q: "Compulsory Heterosexuality and Lesbian Existence" (1980) was written by:
A: Adrienne Rich | Mary Daly | Audre Lorde | Catharine MacKinnon
K: A | Adrienne Rich.

Q: Who proposed International Women's Day at Copenhagen in 1910?
A: Alexandra Kollontai | Clara Zetkin | Rosa Luxemburg | August Bebel
K: B | Clara Zetkin; the UN began marking 8 March in 1975.

Q: *Woman and Socialism* (1879) was written by:
A: Friedrich Engels | August Bebel | Karl Marx | Lewis Henry Morgan
K: B | August Bebel.

Q: Engels' *Origin of the Family* (1884) drew on the anthropology of:
A: Margaret Mead | Lewis Henry Morgan | Bronisław Malinowski | None of these
K: B | Lewis Henry Morgan's *Ancient Society* (1877).

Q: The Wages for Housework campaign (1972) grew from the work of:
A: Dalla Costa and James | Hartmann and Eisenstein | Mitchell and Rowbotham | None of these
K: A | Mariarosa Dalla Costa and Selma James.

Q: "They say it is love. We say it is unwaged work" was written by:
A: Silvia Federici | Maria Mies | Margaret Benston | Arlie Hochschild

Q: "The Unhappy Marriage of Marxism and Feminism" (1979), the classic of dual-systems theory, was written by:
A: Zillah Eisenstein | Heidi Hartmann | Iris Marion Young | Juliet Mitchell
K: B | Heidi Hartmann.

Q: Juliet Mitchell's *Woman's Estate* (1971) named four structures. Which is NOT one of them?
A: Production | Reproduction | Religion | Socialisation of children
K: C | Production, reproduction, sexuality and socialisation of children.

Q: Sylvia Walby (1990) identified how many structures of patriarchy?
A: Four | Five | Six | Eight
K: C | Six: paid work, household production, culture, sexuality, violence and the state.

Q: Unified-systems theory (1980), in contrast to dual systems, was proposed by:
A: Iris Marion Young | Heidi Hartmann | Sylvia Walby | Nancy Hartsock
K: A | Iris Marion Young.

Q: Who said "Anatomy is destiny"?
