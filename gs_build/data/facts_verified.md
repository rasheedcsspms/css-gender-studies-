## Indices and data ★
- ★ **Global Gender Gap Report 2026** (WEF, released September 2026): Pakistan **143rd of 145**, score **59.5%** (up 2.8
  points; strongest gain in a decade). Sub-indices: economic participation & opportunity **144th** (last in South Asia);
  educational attainment **130th**; political empowerment **107th** (education and politics each up 11 places).
  Lowest of 38 lower-middle-income economies and of the 7 South Asian countries. Global gap **69.2%** closed; parity
  **120 years** away (2146); Iceland 1st (93%), 17th year at the top; 145 economies. India 131st.
  Sources: Dawn (news/2030632), Express Tribune (2629881), WEF press release 2026/09, Business Standard.
- ★ **GGGR 2025**: Pakistan **148th of 148**, score **56.7%** (57% in 2024); women ministers fell from 5.9% (2024) to 0;
  female literacy (as used by WEF) 46.5% → 48.5%; education parity 85.1%. Sources: Express Tribune 2550530; Dawn 1916743.
- GGGR history (Pakistan): 2006 112/115; 2017 143/144 ("second worst", the 2018 exam question); 2018 148/149;
  2021 153/156; 2022 145/146; 2023 142/146 (MCQ 2024); 2024 145/146; 2025 148/148; 2026 143/145.
  (2006–2024 values from memory of the WEF reports — re-check before quoting in the FB.)
- ★ **Labour Force Survey 2024–25** (PBS): female LFPR **24.4%** (21.4% in 2020–21); under the 19th ICLS definition
  22.7%; labour force 85.6 million (71.8m in 2020–21). Provincial female LFPR: Punjab 33.8%, KP 21.1%, Sindh 20.5%,
  Balochistan 14.5%. Source: PBS LFS 2024-25 Annual Report; Gallup Pakistan briefs.
- **Census 2023** (PBS): literacy **60.65%**; men ~**68%**, women ~**53%** (52.8%).
- **Maternal mortality ratio 186** per 100,000 live births — Pakistan Maternal Mortality Survey 2019 (NIPS).
- **GSMA Mobile Gender Gap** (as stated in CSS 2026 Q8a): mobile-internet gender gap fell from **38% to 25%**; eight
  million more women online (2024–2025).
- WHO (2021): about **1 in 3** women (≈736 million) have experienced physical/sexual violence in their lifetime.
- UNFPA (2022 floods): about **650,000** pregnant women in flood-affected areas.

## Law ★
- **ICT Child Marriage Restraint Act 2025** — NA passed 16 May 2025; Senate 19 May; presidential assent **30 May
  2025**; minimum age **18** for both sexes in Islamabad; nikah khwan who solemnises: up to 1 year + Rs100,000; bill
  moved by MNA Sharmila Faruqi; opposed by CII and JUI-F. Sources: Dawn 1914444; Library of Congress Global Legal Monitor.
- Sindh Child Marriages Restraint Act **2013** (18). **Punjab Assembly passed a Punjab Child Marriage Restriction Bill
  in April 2026** (UN Women Pakistan bulletin 2025–26); Balochistan also reported — verify exact title/date for FB.
- **ICT Domestic Violence (Prevention and Protection) Act 2025** (UN Women Pakistan bulletin 2025–26) — verify date.
- **2026 NA bills** for 33% women on federal boards/statutory bodies (Business Recorder 40424952).
- **Transgender Persons (Protection of Rights) Act 2018**; **FSC judgment 19 May 2023** held ss. 2(f), 3 and 7 (and
  related) repugnant to Islam (self-perceived identity; inheritance). Appeal by Farhatullah Babar (PPP, July 2023) and
  others before the **Shariat Appellate Bench of the Supreme Court — still pending in 2026** (VOJ News, May 2026).
  Under Art. 203D(3), an FSC decision under appeal does not take effect until the appeal is decided.
- Supreme Court 2009 (Dr Muhammad Aslam Khaki v. SSP Rawalpindi): recognition of khawaja sira; third-gender CNIC.

## Topic 6 — governance and quotas ★ (checked October 2026)
- **Voters 2024** (ECP/FAFEN): 128.58m registered — 69.26m men (53.9%), 59.32m women (46.1%); registration gap **9.94m (7.7%)**,
  down from 12.8% in 2013. Turnout: women **43%**, men **52%**; women cast **24.4m** of 58.9m votes (+2.7m on 2018);
  turnout gap 10 points (2018) → 9 (2024); KP gap widest (15.7 points), Sindh 7.4. Sources: Dawn 1877967; FAFEN GE-2024 brief.
- **Women on general NA seats**: 2013 — 5 at the general election (10 with by-elections); 2018 — **8**; 2024 — **11** (306 contested).
- **Reserved-seat history**: 1956 — 10 seats (5 East, 5 West); 1962 — 6 seats (3+3); 1973 — 10 seats for 10 years or three
  elections; 1985 — raised to **20**; lapsed after 1988 (none in the 1990s assemblies); **2002** (LFO) — **60** NA, 17 Senate,
  128 provincial (now 132 after FATA merger: Punjab 66, Sindh 29, KP 26, Balochistan 11); local 33% (LGO 2001).
  Sources: Wikipedia "Women in Pakistani politics"; PILDAT; IDEA.
- **Senate**: 96 seats, 17 for women (4 per province + 1 ICT); 18 women senators in 2024 (IPU).
- **Bangladesh 2026** (12 Feb): 85 women contested, **7** directly elected; **50** reserved seats (since 2011) filled May 2026;
  57 women in 350. (IPU, IFES.)
- **Nepal 2026** (5 March): women **96 of 275** (34.9%); only **14** via FPTP; constitution 2015 requires 33%. (IPU; Daily Pioneer.)
- **Sri Lanka 2024**: record **22** women MPs (of 225); **Harini Amarasuriya** PM (Sept 2024), third woman PM. 25% local quota (2017).
- **India**: Constitution (106th Amendment) Act **2023** (Nari Shakti Vandan Adhiniyam) — one-third of Lok Sabha and state
  assembly seats, horizontal within SC/ST seats, for 15 years; operates only after the next census and delimitation
  (not before 2029). 73rd/74th Amendments 1992 — one-third of panchayat seats.
